Retrieval-augmented generation

Watch the system choose what to paste.

Every stage below is live. Change a chunk size, move a threshold, ask a different question — the retrieval recomputes and the map redraws. Nothing here is pre-recorded.

1 — Your sources

The knowledge base. The model knows nothing about any of this until a chunk from here lands in a prompt. Delete a source and that knowledge disappears instantly — no retraining.

2 — Chunking

Documents get cut into pieces, because you retrieve pieces, not whole files. Chunk size is a real product trade-off: too small and a chunk loses the context that made it meaningful, too large and you drag noise into the prompt.

240 chars
Try this: drag the slider to 80, then jump to stage 5 and look at what gets retrieved. Then try 600. Same question, different answer quality — and you never touched the model.

3 — Embedding

Each chunk becomes a list of numbers — a position on a map of meaning. This happens once, ahead of time, for every chunk. At question time nothing here is re-read.

Honesty note: a real embedding model produces ~1,536 dimensions that nobody can name, learned from enormous amounts of text. This simulator uses 8 hand-defined badminton concepts so you can actually see the numbers. The mechanism — text becomes a vector, vectors get compared by angle — is identical.

4 — The question

Only the question gets converted now. Everything else was positioned days ago. This is why retrieval across millions of chunks returns in under a second — it isn't reading, it's comparing coordinates.

5 — Retrieval

Distance is measured from the question to every chunk. The closest few win. Two knobs here are pure product decisions, not engineering ones.

3
0.15
The floor is the whole design decision. Below it, a chunk is discarded even if it was the closest one available. Raise it and the system starts saying "I don't know." Lower it to zero and it will always find something to say — confidently, and sometimes about the wrong player. That's the NotebookLM choice, in one slider.

6 — The assembled prompt

This is the entire point of RAG. Not a clever technique — just this string, built automatically, a moment before the model sees it.
